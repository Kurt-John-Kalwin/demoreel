# Design: Demoreel, narrated demo videos as a build artifact

## Problem Statement

Every shipped thing owes the world a demo video and almost nobody makes one. Recording takes hours, people hate their own voice, the video is stale the moment the UI changes, and reviewers of agent-written PRs cannot see what changed without running it. Kurt has this problem five times over in his own repos (App Review asked him for a screen recording on 27 Aug; the Top Sets app-preview script from 26 Aug was never rendered; the topsets.app landing page hand-codes a ghost cursor because no real video exists; Fact Drop's launch plan says "Twitter thread with demo video"; releasedeck, clonecheck and the SDK findings have no launch clip).

Demoreel makes the demo a build artifact. Give it a URL and one sentence and a stealth Solari cloud browser walks the product, a narration is written and voiced, and a 1080p video with a synthesized cursor and captions comes back in a couple of minutes. Keep the storyboard in the repo as `demoreel.yaml` and a GitHub Action re-renders it from every preview deployment, so the video never goes stale and every PR gets a clip.

## Demand Evidence

- "5 hours to create the final video" (HN); "So the demo just...doesn't happen" (DemoFly founders); "teams either rush it or skip it" (BuildShot); "we'd ship a product update that made them all outdated" (Ottoclip). Full quotes with links in the research digest, section 3.
- The market validated the thesis at both ends in 2026: Clueso Agents (enterprise, "Connect GitHub, and Clueso updates your videos the moment you ship") and DemoFly (free local Claude plugin, "Regenerate any scene in seconds"). Both charge or gate on the same pain.
- Reviewer demand in the agent era: Haystack marks a PR safe to merge when it "includes a screenshot showing the final state"; Ito sells "screenshots, videos, and run logs".
- First-party: six separate video needs in Kurt's own repos in the last ten days, zero videos produced.

What is missing from the evidence: Reddit and X were unreachable this session, and nobody has yet been watched using a paste-a-URL flow. That is the assignment.

## Status Quo

QuickTime or OBS, several takes, a voiceover or silence, ffmpeg or iMovie, then Screen Studio at $20-29/mo for the polish, paused between launches. Or Fiverr. Or, as on topsets.app, a CSS animation pretending to be a video. For PRs: a screenshot if the author remembered, otherwise "trust me".

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The human: Kurt, shipping Top Sets (web at topsets.app, Next.js) and connexa (Next.js on Vercel) and three challenge tools, who needs a launch clip this week and has none. Behind him: the DemoFly and BuildShot founders' persona, "engineers who'd rather ship than record", and the reviewer of agent-written PRs who wants to watch the change instead of reading the diff.

Narrowest wedge: **paste a public URL and one sentence, get a 30-60 second narrated 1080p demo of that page, with a share link.** No login pages, no checkout, no install, no account. That single flow is the launch surface and the usage proof. The repo file and the GitHub Action are the second week, not the first.

## Constraints

Verified this session unless noted.

- Solari capture: Playwright `recordVideo` works against the remote Playwright server at 1080p25 (webm, VP8), delivered via `video.saveAs()`; no cursor in the footage. rrweb replay is DOM data, not video, and its endpoint had a multi-hour outage on 2 Sep (rushes' runs). Remote CDP screencast is 1 fps from outside the region. So: footage from `recordVideo`, cursor and zoom composited from our own action log.
- Sandbox `base`: Node 18, no ffmpeg (apt install 50 s, then snapshot and promote to a template), 1 vCPU regardless of the `cpu` request, 7 ms to the gateway.
- Plan: Starter $20 credits, 20 concurrent browsers, 2 VMs, 5 h max session; browser $0.10/h. A 60 s demo costs about $0.003 of browser time; the LLM plan and TTS dominate at roughly $0.02-0.04 per video. $20 funds 400-600 public videos.
- Public-door abuse: a free URL-in video-out service is a scraper and a spam target. Public http(s) only, no private IP ranges (SSRF), honor robots.txt, refuse pages that look like login, checkout or account pages, per-IP daily cap, global daily credit budget with a hard kill, 90 s cap per video, single-flight queue with a visible position.
- Third-party pages are rendered inside our video with narration we generated. Show the source URL and timestamp on the share page and let a site owner request takedown by email. Do not render pages whose robots.txt disallows us.
- Rate limits: this session lost four subagents to the account limit. Build with tests that run offline (fixtures for the planner, fake TTS, fake browser) so development does not depend on live quota.

## Premises

1. The narrated-demo lane in the challenge field is empty: rushes pivoted to receipts and never shipped a video, voice or planner; no other entry touches it. (Verified 3 Sep, GitHub search plus 39 cookbook PRs.)
2. Collision with commercial tools is acceptable under Harry's rubric; what matters is that Kurt has the problem and strangers use the fix. DemoFly and Clueso Agents prove buyers exist and leave the self-serve, cloud, CI-native, script-in-git middle open.
3. Solari is load-bearing, not decorative: the stealth cloud browser is what lets a public service demo any site without an install, and per-PR renders from CI need a browser that is not on the runner. Remove Solari and the product collapses to DemoFly.
4. Narration-first pacing (voice the script, then drive the browser with dwell times that match each clip) gives perfectly synced voice without an editor. Nobody in the field or the market advertises this; it is the trick that makes the output watchable on the first render. (Unverified until Day 0.)
5. Planner quality on arbitrary pages is the biggest technical risk. The v1 planner "tours a page": scroll, top-level navigation, hovers, at most two clicks deep, and it exposes the storyboard for editing. Site owners who care write the storyboard by hand once; that is the demo-as-code product.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Public web app only (minimal viable)
Summary: paste URL + sentence, queue, render, share page. One always-on Node worker with ffmpeg, Vercel for the front end.
Effort: M (human ~4 days / CC ~1.5 days). Risk: Medium (planner quality).
Pros: fastest path to strangers using it; the launch video can be made by the product; smallest surface.
Cons: no retention, a toy once the launch bump fades; nothing in the user's repo; nothing for PRs.
Reuses: probe scripts, Solari SDK, ffmpeg-static.

### Approach B: Demo-as-code pipeline with two doors (CHOSEN)
Summary: one pipeline (plan, drive, narrate, render, publish) behind two doors: the public web app (traction) and the `demoreel` CLI plus GitHub Action that renders `demoreel.yaml` from a preview URL on every PR or deploy and posts a GIF plus link on the PR (retention, the moat).
Effort: L (human ~2 weeks / CC ~5-6 days). Risk: Medium.
Pros: solves Kurt's own recurring need on his own repos; the storyboard in git is what makes videos never go stale, which is the validated pain; PR clips are unserved and timely; localization is a `lang:` list once narration is text.
Cons: two surfaces to keep honest; GitHub cannot take mp4 uploads via API, so the PR comment carries a GIF and a link; more moving parts to guard.
Reuses: everything in A; Playwright fixtures pattern from Twenty Tabs eng review; cost accountant and kill switch design from the ask-human doc.

### Approach C: PR-clips only, no public door (lateral)
Summary: a GitHub App: install, and every PR with a Vercel preview gets a narrated clip. No web app.
Effort: M. Risk: Medium-High (distribution: nobody installs an app from a tweet; the launch post has no link a stranger can click).
Pros: sharpest single job, most defensible.
Cons: fails Harry's "get people to use it" in the first 72 hours; Kurt's launch clips still need a manual path.

## Recommended Approach

Approach B, built in the order A then the Action: the public door ships first because it is the usage proof and the launch asset, and the Action ships on the same pipeline days later because it is the reason anyone comes back. DECISION 1 for Kurt: agree with the order, or flip to Action-first if the challenge post can wait a week.

Pipeline (one module each, all testable offline):

1. **plan**: URL + sentence (+ optional PR title/body and diff paths) -> storyboard. Inputs to the LLM: page text, a 1280x800 screenshot, the nav links. Output: 4-8 scenes, each `say` (narration, 8-25 words) and `do` (goto, scroll_to, hover, click, type, wait). The storyboard is JSON with a YAML mirror for repos.
2. **narrate**: TTS per scene first. Default voice via an API (DECISION 2: OpenAI TTS for quality and simplicity, or Kokoro inside a Solari sandbox for $0; ship with an adapter interface and pick after the Day 0 listen test). Measure each clip's duration.
3. **drive**: Solari stealth browser, `newContext({ recordVideo: 1920x1080 })`, execute each scene's actions, dwell until the scene's narration would end, log every action with timestamps, coordinates and target bounding boxes relative to context creation.
4. **render**: ffmpeg-static: footage + cursor overlay from the action log (eased path, click ripple) + optional zoom on click targets (v1.5, off by default because HN users find constant zoom dizzying) + captions (ASS) + intro and outro cards + mixed narration. Emit mp4 (H.264), webm, a GIF for PR comments, VTT captions and `storyboard.json`.
5. **publish**: object storage (Cloudflare R2, Kurt already has Cloudflare) and a share page `/v/{id}` with the video, the storyboard, the source URL, the timestamp and download links. The PR comment embeds the GIF and links the page.

Where it runs: the web front end on Vercel; **one always-on worker** (Fly.io or Railway machine, 2 GB) with an in-memory queue and SQLite, running the whole pipeline with ffmpeg local. The GitHub Action runs the same CLI on the runner. Rendering inside a Solari sandbox from a baked template is a v1.5 scale-out and a talking point, not a v1 dependency (the eng review of Twenty Tabs killed a five-service plan for the same reason). DECISION 3: Fly.io vs Railway vs a Cloudflare Container for the worker; recommend Fly.io for the simplest ffmpeg box.

## Open Questions

1. Day 0 sync gate: what is the offset between Playwright video frame 0 and our action clock, and does it drift over 60 s? Target under 150 ms. If it drifts, insert a visual sync marker at the start and detect it in ffmpeg.
2. Day 0 listen test: is the narration good enough to put on topsets.app? Compare three voices from the chosen provider and one Kokoro render.
3. Planner fixtures: which 20 public pages form the offline test set (landing pages, docs, dashboards with public demos, a bot-hostile site through stealth)?
4. Does `expo start --web` render enough of Top Sets to demo the app itself, or is topsets.app the only Kurt target? (Unknown; Skia and MMKV may not run on web.)
5. GIF size in PR comments: GitHub renders images through camo with a size cap; target under 8 MB, 8 s, 720p.
6. Name: `demoreel` is free on npm and unique enough on GitHub (only unrelated Fibonacci LED repos). DECISION 4: keep Demoreel, or pick a name that says "never stale" (stalefree and freshdemo are also free).

## Success Criteria

1. The launch video on X is a Demoreel render of Demoreel's own share page (the promise rushes made and never delivered).
2. 72 hours after posting: at least 50 videos rendered for at least 30 distinct IPs other than Kurt's, shown by a live counter on the landing page.
3. At least three repos not owned by Kurt have the Action posting clips on PRs, visible publicly.
4. Kurt's own backlog cleared: topsets.app landing video, connexa, releasedeck, clonecheck launch clips, all rendered by the tool.
5. Cost per public video at or under $0.05; render time at or under 3 minutes for a 45 s demo; zero renders of disallowed pages.

## What I noticed about how you think

- "Let us iterate on demo reel" is the second time you have come back to a play that was killed by a collision rather than by evidence. Under Harry's rubric that instinct was right both times.
- "The advantage between this product should be huge" sets the bar at the output, not the feature list; the two things that make the output different are narration-first sync and the storyboard living in git, and both are cheap.
- "Research on the problems in real life issues maybe like from X from Reddit" is the correct order of operations; this session could not reach either, and the design says so rather than pretending.
- You ship mobile apps and hand-code fake walkthroughs on your own landing page. You are the first user, and the first success criterion is your own backlog, not a stranger's.
